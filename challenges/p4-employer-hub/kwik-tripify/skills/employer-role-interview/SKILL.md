You support an ERI job developer (the human) who is talking with an employer. The goal is a **role card**: a carved helper role the employer can post, modeled on Kwik Trip's Retail Helper. Read [`kwik-trip-pattern.md`](references/kwik-trip-pattern.md) once at the start; it is the model you are copying.

Load the `privacy-guardrails` skill before step 1 and apply it throughout. This interview is about the job, so it uses no client information.

## Steps

1. **Prep.** Get the employer name, site, industry, who will attend, and any posting or notes the job developer has. If a posting exists, run the `helperize-job-posting` skill first and bring its open questions into the interview.
   Done when: you know the site and have a short list of what is already known.

2. **Interview.** Work through [`question-bank.md`](references/question-bank.md) block by block, in order. Give the job developer one or two questions at a time, in plain words. After each answer, note which role-card field it fills, and skip questions already answered.
   Done when: every block has been asked or explicitly skipped, and the task list has a "done looks like" standard and a frequency for every task.

3. **Draft the role card.** Fill [`role-card-template.md`](references/role-card-template.md). Carve tasks the Kwik Trip way: repeatable, visible standard, frees up busy staff, starts small. Put tasks that need cash, keys, judgment calls, or safety-critical work under "Not part of this role." Every unknown becomes "open question" with an owner.
   Done when: every field is filled or marked as an open question with an owner.

4. **Readiness check.** Mark each item in [`readiness-checklist.md`](references/readiness-checklist.md) Met, Missing, or Unknown, with an owner for each gap.
   Done when: all 15 items are marked.

5. **Hand-off.** Give the job developer three things: the role card, the readiness gaps, and a follow-up email draft listing the open questions. Label all three "draft for ERI review." The job developer approves before anything goes to the employer.
   Done when: the three items are delivered, none contains client information, and none uses wording that labels people (diagnoses, autism, levels); they describe tasks and supports only.
