# Executive-function demand rubric

Rate the **task**, not a person: 0 means the task has none of this demand; then 1 low, 2 medium, 3 high per the anchors. When a task sits between two anchors, pick the higher one. The supports column lists matching accommodations; most come from [JAN's executive functioning page](https://AskJAN.org/articles/Executive-Functioning-Deficits.cfm) and [JAN autism page](https://AskJAN.org/disabilities/Autism-Spectrum.cfm). JAN does not list `flexibility` as its own category; it is ERI's addition.

| Key | Dimension | 1 low | 2 medium | 3 high | Supports |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `working_memory` | Remembering steps and instructions | One or two steps, same each time | 3-7 steps, or instructions given once verbally | Many steps held in mind, or new verbal instructions each shift | Picture or written checklist at the station; labels; extra training time |
| `sequencing` | Doing steps in order, juggling tasks | One task at a time, fixed order | Switches between 2-3 tasks in a set routine | Many tasks at once, order changes with demand | One task at a time; labeled flowchart; supervisor sets order |
| `time_management` | Pacing, deadlines, estimating time | No time pressure; done any time in the shift | Set times for tasks (every hour, before lunch, at close) | Tight or shifting deadlines, rush periods | Timer or phone alarm; task chunking; visual schedule |
| `attention` | Staying on task amid distraction | Quiet area, short tasks | Some interruptions or a long task | Constant interruptions, vigilance needed | Quieter area; headphones where safe; scheduled breaks |
| `organization` | Ranking, finding, and keeping things in order | Fixed supply spots, one priority | Some prioritizing or restocking choices | Decides own priorities across many items | Color-coding; supervisor-set priorities; fixed supply locations |
| `flexibility` | Handling change in routine | Same routine daily | Occasional changes with notice | Frequent unplanned changes | Advance notice of changes; written change card; one go-to person |
| `social_communication` | Talking with coworkers and customers | Little talk; nods and greetings | Routine questions from customers or coworkers | Complaints, negotiation, or reading subtle cues | Scripts for common questions; a buddy to hand off to; consistent feedback |
| `sensory_load` | Noise, light, crowding, temperature | Calm, steady conditions | Some noise, crowding, or cold or heat | Loud, crowded, or constant alarms | Headphones; lighting options; breaks in a calm area |
| `paperwork` | Forms, logs, reading and writing | None, or a checkbox | Short logs or labels to read | Forms, reports, or detailed reading | Pre-filled forms; picture logs; templates |

## Support intensity rule (role level)

- **light**: at most one dimension rated 2, none rated 3. Standard onboarding plus a checklist.
- **moderate**: two to four dimensions rated 2, none rated 3. Job coach on site for the first weeks; visual supports; check-ins every 2 weeks.
- **intensive**: any 3, or five or more 2s. Carve further before posting, or plan ongoing coaching.

Intensity describes the **role**. It says how much support the role needs, never who is allowed to apply.
