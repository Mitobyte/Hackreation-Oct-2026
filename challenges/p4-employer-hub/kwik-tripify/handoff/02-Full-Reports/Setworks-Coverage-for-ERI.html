Setworks Coverage: What It Already Solves for ERI

2026-10-31 · Paul Rinaldi

Bottom line

Setworks fully covers none of ERI's 12 problems and none of the 24 solutions. It partly covers 6 problems and 9 solutions, almost all on the staff back-office side. It looks like a real fit for reporting, case notes, employer and job records, and milestone billing. It does not appear to cover anything workers, families, or employers touch. That includes all of P1–P3, benefits counseling (H1), and the AI writing tools (#22 resume, #23 job ad).

The top hackathon pick, the local paperwork and match pipeline (#21–#24), is still mostly open. Two points need checking in the demo:

  • #21 Template Autofill: an independent audit found that Setworks can merge a client's form data into a Word or Excel template, one form at a time (help article). No bulk run or CSV input was found. So #21 still matters for bulk work and for ERI's templates outside Setworks, but it is less unique than we thought.

  • #24 Ad–resume matcher: the audit found no matching method at all, only stored employers and jobs. The matcher is still a clear gap.

How reliable is this? Two Sonnet subagents researched Setworks on Oct 31, 2026. The second pass audited the first and read about 20 help-center articles. It raised confidence on reporting and templates, downgraded three ratings that had no evidence (#24 matcher, #12 map, #6 fade), and found a paid "AI Enhanced" package and new custom "AI Agents" (2025). Most "not covered" ratings still mean "not found in public pages" (low confidence). Every Setworks fact is a vendor claim unless marked otherwise; the live demo is the real test.

Gaps Setworks leaves

Setworks is a staff back office. The hackathon should build what workers, families, and employers touch, plus anything Wisconsin-specific that Setworks cannot match.

  1. Skills-to-tasks matching (#24). The audit found no matching method in Setworks. A transparent matcher that puts the client's preference first is the clearest gap.

  2. AI writing for people, not notes (#22, #23). Setworks AI drafts and checks case notes. Its new custom agents are not described, so ask whether they can draft resumes or job ads.

  3. Worker-facing tools (#1, #3, #4, #5). Video skill profiles, step cards, and the "I'm stuck" ladder have no Setworks match. Outside people can only sign forms.

  4. Benefits counseling (H1, #9). No WIPA or SSA verification workflow was found. A custom form could track it.

  5. Bulk and outside-Setworks paperwork (#21). Setworks fills Word templates one form at a time from its own data. Bulk runs, CSV input, and templates for other systems are still open.

What not to build: milestone billing, general reporting, or single-form Word output. Setworks documents these. Confirm the employer map (#12) in the demo before deciding.

Path: Setworks export plus a coach-approved AI assistant

Set up Setworks to hold the matching data and export it. Then run an assistant that can only read and draft, with every output waiting for the assigned job coach to approve. Setworks AI searches, drafts, and reviews notes, and its 2025 custom agents are not yet described. So this plan puts the agent outside Setworks, and Setworks stays the record of truth.

1. Configure Setworks (ERI admin, before go-live)

  • Add custom fields that Setworks does not show today:

    • Person: skills shown, interests, preferred hours, bus access, support level, and executive-function supports (reminders, picture steps, check-ins).

    • Job (on job analysis): a task list, with the executive-function demand per task (sequence length, changes per shift, need to ask for help).

  • Save one report per use: client profiles, job analyses, open jobs, placements, and visit notes due. Use client IDs, not names, in exports.

  • Use field-level permissions so that benefits data is not in these exports.

2. Export (verify in the demo)

  • Best: an API with read access per report. Next best: scheduled CSV or Excel exports to a secure ERI folder (SFTP or a shared drive). Last resort: a staff member clicks Export once a day.

  • Ask for import too, so that approved items can go back in without retyping.

3. Local bridge (ERI's PHP team or a small Python script)

  • Loads each export into a local database on an ERI Mac or server. Keeps the ID-to-name map in a separate, locked file.

  • Logs every load, so that staff can see what data the assistant had.

4. The assistant (agent)

  • Recommended: a local open-weights model (for example Qwen, run with Ollama) on an ERI machine, with tool calling. No client data leaves ERI.

  • Alternative: a stronger cloud model (for example Claude through its API), but only with a signed BAA and de-identified data. Check the current terms with the vendor.

  • Tools it gets: read tools (search people, jobs, placements); draft tools (match list with reasons, fill a Word template, draft a resume, cover letter, job ad, or case note).

  • Tools it never gets: write access to Setworks, email to employers, or anything about benefits decisions.

Check first: Setworks added custom "AI Agents" in 2025, and some AI is in a paid "AI Enhanced" package (2025 Year in Review). If those agents can be limited to reading and drafting, with a job coach's approval, ERI may not need a separate agent for work inside Setworks.

5. Approval queue

  • Every draft or proposed action goes into a queue page with its reasons and the source records it used.

  • Only the client's assigned job coach can approve, edit, or reject. Resumes and letters also need a "client reviewed" check. Anything that touches hours or wages goes to the benefits counselor instead.

  • Each decision is logged with who, when, and what changed.

6. After approval

  • The tool produces the Word or PDF file, or writes an import file that goes back into Setworks.

  • Nothing reaches an employer, a family, or Setworks until a coach has approved it.

Pilot: run it with 5 fake clients and 5 fake jobs first. Measure the minutes per document, by hand vs. with approval, before using any real data.

What to verify in the Setworks demo

These checks target what the research could not confirm. Each answer changes what the hackathon should build.

  • Show a Word template filled from a client form. Can it run for 50 clients at once, or take a CSV? (Decides how much of #21 we build.)

  • Build a Wisconsin DVR form live, like the Washington DVR report. Who builds it, at what cost, how fast?

  • Can custom fields go on job analysis and employer records (skills, tasks, executive-function demands), not only on forms?

  • How does job search or "eMaps" pick candidates? Can staff see why?

  • What can the 2025 custom "AI Agents" do? Can they draft resumes or job ads? Can they be limited to drafting with a job coach's approval?

  • What is in the paid "AI Enhanced" package, and what does it cost?

  • Is there an API, or CSV import beyond invoice payments? How do we get data out on a schedule?

  • Can ERI add a prompt-level field and chart fading over time?

  • Is there any benefits counseling or WIPA workflow, such as tracking SSA verification?

  • Is there a native app, voice dictation, or offline notes at worksites?

  • Full price: per-person tiers, setup fee, EVV and AI add-ons. Setup time?

  • Can we call Aptiv (Wisconsin) as a reference, and see its reports?
